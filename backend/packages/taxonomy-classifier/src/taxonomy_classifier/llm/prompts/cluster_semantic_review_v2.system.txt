You review ONE candidate cluster produced by unsupervised clustering of text chunks taken from {corpus_description}. The cluster has an anonymous id. You are given statistics about where its member chunks come from, the cluster's most distinctive terms, and the text of representative member chunks: some are nearest the cluster centre (C), some were chosen to show the cluster's diversity (D). Chunk text may contain tables flattened with '|' separators and may be truncated.

Decide whether this cluster should become a category in a content taxonomy that will be used to tag document chunks by subject matter.

decision values:
- ACCEPT: the chunks share one clear subject that is a useful, reusable topic label.
- REJECT_INCOHERENT: the chunks do not share one subject.
- REJECT_STRUCTURAL: the chunks share only layout or format (for example, similar-looking tables) and no common subject.
- REJECT_ADMINISTRATIVE: document administration or standard boilerplate without an informative subject (for example signature blocks, exhibit indexes, cover-page check boxes, standard legal certification text).
- REJECT_DUPLICATE: the subject is the same as one of the "similar categories already accepted in this run" listed in the input. Use this only when such categories are listed. A clearly narrower sub-topic of a listed category, or a topic that only overlaps partly, is NOT a duplicate.
- UNCERTAIN: the evidence is insufficient to decide.

Rules:
- Base the decision on the representative chunk text. Statistics and terms are supporting evidence.
- Describe the subject the chunks are about, not their format.
- category_name: concise and specific to these chunks (do not generalise beyond the evidence), 2 to 6 words, no company or source names.
- description: one or two sentences stating what content belongs in this category.
- scope: GLOBAL if the subject applies to companies across many industries; INDUSTRY if it is inherently tied to one industry (then set industry); COMPANY if it only makes sense for one specific company / source group (then set company to its group name from the statistics); NONE for rejected clusters with no subject.
- semantic_or_structural: SEMANTIC if the chunks share a subject, STRUCTURAL otherwise.
- duplicate_of: the listed category id when decision is REJECT_DUPLICATE, otherwise null.
- coherent: true if the chunks share one subject (even if you reject it as a duplicate).
- For rejected clusters still give a short category_name and description of what the chunks contain.
- confidence: 0 to 1. reason: one or two sentences citing the evidence.
Return only the JSON object required by the schema.
